# LinkedIn and Cover Letters

## Document altitude

Each document has one job. Mixing them is the most common way a senior candidate reads junior.

| Document | Job | Holds | Leaves out |
|---|---|---|---|
| Resume | Evidence | Proof per bullet, locked figures, keywords where backed | Interpretation, motivation |
| LinkedIn | Altitude | Scope and outcomes at the level you want, function in the headline | Operator detail, client-identifying specifics |
| Cover letter | Interpretation | Why your experience matters for this mandate | A credential recap, operational detail |

## LinkedIn

- **Keep it lean and high-altitude.** It is read by more people, for longer, than any resume, including people at your current employer.
- **The headline carries function; the title field carries the official title.** Never put a title you did not hold in the title field.
- **Confidential specifics stay vaguer than on the resume.** Clients unnamed; figures as scope ("eight-figure portfolio") unless `locked_truth.md` allows them.
- **Open to Work set to recruiters only** if your employer must not see the search.
- **Altitude rises by removing operator detail**, the same rule as resume section B.

## Cover letters

Write one only when asked, or for a Tier 1 role where a human will clearly read it.

1. **Lead with interpretation.** The first paragraph says what problem you think the role exists to solve, and why your experience bears on it. Not "I am excited to apply," and not a summary of your resume.
2. **One or two proofs**, chosen for this mandate, at their locked figures. Never a figure the resume does not also carry.
3. **The transition, framed structurally** when you are changing lanes: what the move makes possible, never what you are escaping.
4. **Personal motivation is allowed** when it is true and short. It must still lead to the role's problem.
5. **Same voice rules:** point first, no em dashes, no filler.

**Check:** every figure in the letter matches the resume and `locked_truth.md`. In the original search, one submitted letter carried a program figure that did not match its own resume. The letter is the document least likely to be checked and most likely to be read closely in a final round.
