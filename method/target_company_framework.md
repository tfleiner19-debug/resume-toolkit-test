# Target Company Framework

This document governs what enters the funnel. `CLAUDE.md` Section 2 governs what happens once a posting is in it.

---

## 1. The reframe

The company list is the cheap part. What breaks the funnel is that a posting's title and vocabulary do not tell you what the seat does, and job-board recommendations match on exactly those words.

In the original search, three screens in one day failed the same way. The candidate's vocabulary sat in each posting, while each seat's main activity was a discipline the candidate had never practiced: ad sales, product design, and statistical modeling.

One variable predicted all three before the JD was read: **which organization the function reports into.** The reporting line is a stable fact about a company. It can be checked before any posting exists. So qualification happens once, at company-and-function level, and monitoring happens continuously, at posting level. Two layers, two cadences.

**For you:** write down the two or three organizations your function fits under, and the ones it looks like it fits under but does not. That pair is the most useful line in `profile/tracks.md`.

---

## 2. The qualification gate

Score each candidate company 0 or 1 on five tests. Four or five puts it on the list. Adapt the wording to your function; keep the structure.

1. **Funded function.** At least two people carry your function's titles there, and at least one sits at your target level or above. One person is a hire. Three is a function with a ladder. Check with a LinkedIn people search filtered to the company.
2. **Right reporting line.** The function sits under an organization you fit (section 1). Score 0 if it sits under one you do not.
3. **Does your kind of work.** Evidence that the seat does what you do, not an adjacent discipline: vendor case studies, conference talks, team members' backgrounds, past postings.
4. **Scale and type.** The company size, industry and business model your profile converts in. Note any standing hard gap here, such as B2B-only or a regulated industry.
5. **Reachable.** Fits your location and work-mode rules.

### Automatic disqualifiers, which override the score

Write three or four for your function. Pattern: the function sits inside an organization you do not fit; the last senior posting for the function required a hard gap as a stated minimum; no standing function exists because the work is outsourced.

### The check that does the most work

**Read the last closed posting for that function, even though it is closed.** The archived JD names the bar the company sets for that seat, months before the next opening exists. It is how a company gets correctly classified as "target company, no target job."

## 3. Search vocabulary

The sourcing problem in one line: level plus a broad word surfaces seats owned by other disciplines.

- **Search on** narrow, function-specific terms with a low false-positive rate.
- **Do not search on** broad terms that another discipline owns. Each one reintroduces the false positives the gate removes.
- **Level words:** your target level and one above.

Record both lists in `profile/tracks.md`. Flag any term that is a coin flip, where the reporting line decides it.

---

## 4. Pre-screen, before any full read

Three questions, answered from the JD alone, in under a minute:

1. What does the person in this seat do most of the time?
2. Who do they report to, and what organization is that?
3. Does the seat do your kind of work, or the adjacent discipline that shares its vocabulary?

A failure on question 2 or 3 is a Tier 3 no-go with a named mechanism, which satisfies the tiering gate without a full read.

---

## 5. Dimensions: where to look, and what each is for

Group target companies into two to four dimensions. They will not carry equal odds, and treating them as equal wastes effort. For each, write down its conversion odds and its ceiling.

The original search used three, which show the pattern:
- **Direct competitors of the current employer.** Highest conversion: the title translates and the experience reads as relevant. Lowest ceiling: it repeats the situation being left. Worth running because it is cheap and a live offer is negotiating position.
- **The destination.** The goal, and the lowest conversion, because a structural barrier (such as a missing title type) screens the resume out. No resume edit removes it. A referral does. Build this list for relationship work first and posting alerts second.
- **Local companies.** Narrow field, fewer applicants per seat, and living locally breaks ties on hybrid roles. Usually underweighted.

**A work-mode preference is a filter, not a dimension.** Applied as a gate inside a local dimension, it can empty the list, so rank by it instead.

### The referral pool

Map people, not accounts. Search your first-degree connections for people whose current employer is on your list and whose title carries a section 3 term. Former colleagues who have moved into your target seats are the warmest contacts you have.

Check your agreements first. A non-solicitation clause can rule out your employer's clients as a target tier, and interviewing inside a team that buys from your employer creates exposure that can reach your employer quickly. In the original search, the client roster was proposed as a warm tier and rightly rejected on both counts.

## 6. Your lists

Keep them in `profile/tracks.md` or a `profile/targets.md` you create, never in this file. For each company: dimension, Q1 to Q5 score, reporting line, last closed posting checked (date), board token if it has a feed.

## 7. What monitoring can and cannot do

See `monitoring/SWEEP_RUNBOOK.md` for the procedure. In summary:

- **Works:** polling public board feeds (Greenhouse, Lever, Workable) for named companies. A feed returns open requisitions only, so liveness is free.
- **Works, discovery only:** web search restricted to job-board domains, using your narrow vocabulary. A hit proves the company has the function. It does not prove the job is open.
- **Does not work, or not reliably:** LinkedIn (login wall), Workday boards (cannot be polled; page readability varies by employer), Ashby and SmartRecruiters feeds (blocked by their robots files), aggregators.

**Coverage, stated honestly:** feeds cover startups and mid-size companies well and large corporates poorly, because large corporates mostly run Workday. LinkedIn covers the inverse. So the manual half never goes away.

---

## 8. Cadence

- **Quarterly:** re-run the gate on new candidates; re-check anchors for a changed reporting line or new function leader.
- **Weekly:** detect openings (runbook). Postings cluster Monday to Wednesday, and a weekly sweep catches one within about five days, well inside a senior search window. Daily adds noise, not opportunities.
- **On you:** LinkedIn alerts set on the narrow vocabulary, never on job-fit recommendations, and a monthly manual pass over the Workday companies on your list.

---

## 9. What monitoring does not fix

Sourcing is a real problem, and this framework fixes it. Conversion into the destination dimension is a different problem. A resume missing the expected title type gets screened out regardless of how well the posting was sourced.

A referral changes that. If the goal is the destination rather than a step up in the current lane, the highest-return hour in a given week goes to finding former colleagues now sitting in target seats, not to the application queue. Monitoring catches the seat. A person gets the resume read.
